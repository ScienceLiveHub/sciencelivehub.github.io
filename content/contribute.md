Most of the work that makes science trustworthy is invisible. Replications sit in
drawers, careful reanalyses never become papers, and peer review is anonymous by design.
On Science Live, that work is **published, signed, and credited to you**.

## What you get

- **Credit that cannot be lost.** Every nanopublication is signed with your key, linked to your ORCID, and permanent. Nobody can remove your name from it.
- **Visibility where it matters.** Anyone looking at a claim sees who made it, who tested it, and what they found. Your replication shows up next to the original work, not in a footnote.
- **Credit for every outcome.** A replication that qualifies or refutes a claim is credited exactly like one that confirms it. Honest results are the point.
- **Open, signed review.** Anyone in the community can approve, disapprove or comment on any nanopublication, at any time, not just once before publication. Each review is itself signed, so careful reviewers get credit instead of staying anonymous.
- **Found by people and machines.** Your work is machine-readable, so tools such as [Replication Radar](/tools/) and AI assistants using our [MCP servers](/tools/) can find it and cite it.
- **A story readers can follow.** Replications can be turned into a [replication story](/#stories): a readable account of your work that links every statement to its evidence.

## How to start

1. **Pick a claim.** Choose one from your own work, or one from the [replication requests](/request-a-replication/) others have asked for.
2. **Do the work in the open.** The [FORRT replication template](https://github.com/ScienceLiveHub/forrt-replication-template) gives you a complete, archived, citable setup.
3. **Publish the chain.** Create your nanopublications on the [platform](https://platform.sciencelive4all.org), or let an AI assistant draft them with the [FORRT Research MCP](/tools/).
