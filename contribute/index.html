Get credited for the work that holds science up

Publish your claims and replications as signed nanopublications. Your name stays on them, permanently and publicly.

Most of the work that makes science trustworthy is invisible. Replications sit in drawers, careful reanalyses never become papers, and peer review is anonymous by design. On Science Live, that work is published, signed, and credited to you.

Your contribution also stays connected as the research moves on. New replications can confirm, qualify or refute a claim at any time, and each one is linked back to the claim and to the question it started from. Here is a real example, from the bumble bee study of Soroye et al. (2020):

Three ways to contribute

Share a claim

Report a finding as a structured, citable claim, without waiting months for a journal. It gets a permanent identifier and your ORCID from day one.

Replicate a claim

Confirm, qualify, or refute a published result. Your replication joins the evidence chain of the claim it tests, under your name. It is not an anonymous review.

Build on others

Reuse claims, datasets, and methods as building blocks. Every reuse is a citation the original author can see.

What you get

  • Credit that cannot be lost. Every nanopublication is signed with your key, linked to your ORCID, and permanent. Nobody can remove your name from it.
  • Visibility where it matters. Anyone looking at a claim sees who made it, who tested it, and what they found. Your replication shows up next to the original work, not in a footnote.
  • Credit for every outcome. A replication that qualifies or refutes a claim is credited exactly like one that confirms it. Honest results are the point.
  • Open, signed review. Anyone in the community can approve, disapprove or comment on any nanopublication, at any time, not just once before publication. Each review is itself signed, so careful reviewers get credit instead of staying anonymous.
  • Found by people and machines. Your work is machine-readable, so tools such as Replication Radar and AI assistants using our MCP servers can find it and cite it.
  • A story readers can follow. Replications can be turned into a replication story: a readable account of your work that links every statement to its evidence.

How to start

  1. Pick a claim. Choose one from your own work, or one from the replication requests others have asked for.
  2. Do the work in the open. The FORRT replication template gives you a complete, archived, citable setup.
  3. Publish the chain. Create your nanopublications on the platform, or let an AI assistant draft them with the FORRT Research MCP.
